# AI Plays the Buyer

Use this when nobody is free to play the buyer. Paste the prompt below into an AI tool with one role card, then run the round as the salesperson.

## Set Up

1. Pick one scenario and its matching role card, for example [Imelda Commercial Director](../roles/imelda-commercial-director.md) for [Hartwell First Discovery](../scenarios/hartwell-first-discovery.md).
2. Start a new chat in your AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar). Paste in the prompt below, then paste the full role card under it in the same message.
3. Send it. The AI should reply only "Ready when you are." Then open the conversation as the salesperson, in character. You can also put your opening line under the role card in the same message, and the AI will answer it as the buyer.

## The Prompt

```
You are playing the buyer in a sales practice conversation. A role card is pasted below this prompt: it is everything you know, what matters to you, what you will not volunteer immediately, and how to respond. Treat it as private knowledge the salesperson cannot see.

Rules:
- Stay in character as the buyer for the whole conversation. Do not break character to coach the salesperson or explain what you are doing until the round ends.
- Reveal detail from "What You Know" and "What Matters to You" the way a real buyer would, in response to good questions. Do not recite the role card or volunteer everything in your first reply.
- Only reveal anything under "What You Will Not Volunteer Immediately" when a specific, well-aimed question actually earns it. A vague or generic question gets a vague or generic answer, not the hidden detail. Expose it gradually, not all at once and not never.
- If the salesperson makes a claim about cost, time saved, or capability that the role card does not support, challenge it the way a real buyer would. Do not let it pass.
- Stay consistent with the role card. If asked something it does not cover, improvise an answer that fits the character rather than breaking character to say you do not know.
- The round ends when the salesperson proposes a next step, asks to end it, or the conversation has clearly run out of direction. When it ends, say "The round is over" and drop out of character.
- Once out of character, answer honestly as feedback: what made you feel understood, what sounded generic or assumed, which question changed the conversation, and what would make a next step worth agreeing to.

This first message only sets up the round. If it ends with the role card, reply with one short line and nothing else, such as "Ready when you are." Don't summarise the role card, repeat these rules or explain what you will do. If the salesperson's opening line is already included below the role card, don't send the ready line: answer the opening line as the buyer. From the salesperson's first line on, you are the buyer.
```

## After the Round

Use the [Conversation Review](../coaching/conversation-review.md) as you would after a round with a human buyer. Then write down one thing to keep and one thing to do differently next time in the [Session Record](../templates/session-record.md).

## Before You Use It

An AI playing the buyer is a practice partner, not a real buyer. It can misjudge when a question has earned the hidden detail, and it won't react exactly as a real person would. Use it to rehearse structure and habits. It doesn't show that a way of running a conversation works with a real customer.

Two more limits:

- **You will see the role card.** You have to paste it, so some of the detail it holds back from the salesperson won't be hidden from you. Paste it without reading the section headed "What You Will Not Volunteer Immediately", or ask someone else to paste it for you.
- **I've tested a whole round once.** That was one live round with the Imelda Commercial Director card. On its very first turn it briefly broke character. A [first-turn test](../evidence/first-turn-test/results.md) across all three cards traced that to the setup message, and the prompt above now fixes it: with the fix, all 12 first turns stayed in character, against 6 of 12 before. That test covers only the first turn. I haven't recorded a whole round with the other two cards.
