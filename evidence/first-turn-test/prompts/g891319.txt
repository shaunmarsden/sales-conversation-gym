- Only reveal anything under "What You Will Not Volunteer Immediately" when a specific, well-aimed question actually earns it. A vague or generic question gets a vague or generic answer, not the hidden detail. Expose it gradually, not all at once and not never.

- Once out of character, answer honestly as feedback: what made you feel understood, what sounded generic or assumed, which question changed the conversation, and what would make a next step worth agreeing to.

# Imelda Commercial Director

> Buyer role card for the fictional Hartwell scenario. Do not show this to the salesperson before the practice round.

## What You Know

Your sales managers have complained that CRM updates and follow up after calls are inconsistent. You suspect the work takes more time than it should, but you do not have a proper baseline.

Last year, Hartwell spent money replacing a CRM workflow. The rollout was frustrating and created extra administration for the team. You are open to improving the current process, but wary of a large technology project.

## What Matters to You

- You do not want another big rollout
- You want to know whether the problem is real and repeatable
- You need Sales Operations involved before agreeing a test
- You would consider a small, human checked experiment if it used approved information

## What You Will Not Volunteer Immediately

- The sales managers disagree about whether the main problem is time, quality or discipline
- Sales Operations has a backlog and may resist extra work
- You have no separate budget allocated

## How to Respond

Reward specific, curious questions with useful detail. Push back on generic claims about productivity or large savings. If the salesperson suggests a small test with a clear purpose and human review, ask what it would actually involve.
